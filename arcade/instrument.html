A little studio.

Play the letters shown in the keyboard map below the device, or use the mouse or a touch screen. Multiple keys make chords. The arrow keys move by octaves. Hold sustains notes; All notes off releases them.

Shape a sound

Synth offers FM bell, detuned saw, pure tone and a sample player. Use the numbered 1–4 buttons to choose Engine, Envelope, Effect or Sequence to change what the four coloured knobs control. Drag a knob vertically, use its slider, or focus the slider beneath the device and use arrow keys. In Tape and Mixer, those numbered buttons select tracks. The eight preset keys select sounds. Sample files stay on your device; C4 plays at the original pitch when Speed is 1×.

Make a loop

Choose tempo and loop length before recording. Press Enter or REC to record onto the selected track. Each pass overdubs audio. Press REC again to finish recording and keep playing. Select another track in Tape to layer another part. Space pauses playback; Stop rewinds. Lift cuts the selected track into a clipboard; Drop overdubs it onto the selected track. Shift + Lift erases, and Shift + Drop undoes. Tape splicing (the scissors button) is not emulated. Undo / redo swaps the most recent recording or erase on its track.

Tape records actual mono audio, including your effects. Playback speed changes pitch and time together; reverse plays backwards. Recording uses normal speed. Mix balances the four tracks. Export WAV saves one loop of the mix, with the track levels and mute settings applied. Tape is held in memory and is lost when you leave or reload.

Drums & sequence

Drum mode repeats eight synthesized percussion voices across the keys: kick, snare, closed hat, open hat, low tom, high tom, clap and metal. Sequence enables an ascending sixteenth-note arpeggiator for held notes, synced to the tempo. Hold keeps the chord going after you release the keys.

This is an independent browser instrument, not the manufacturer's firmware. FM bell and detuned saw adapt open-source op1r custom engines; other engines and tape behaviour are approximations. Source and credits.
