Source & credits

An independent browser instrument inspired by the layout and workflow of the original Teenage Engineering OP-1. No manufacturer logo, firmware, factory samples or original display artwork is included. This project is not affiliated with Teenage Engineering.

What is emulated?

The six-voice FM bell and detuned saw adapt the DSP algorithms of op1r, custom firmware by pcppcp that runs on the original hardware. They are custom engines, not reproductions of the stock engines. The browser port retains their phase arithmetic, parameter ranges, FM feedback and saw morph/drive structure, while providing its own envelope and output path. It is not bit-exact hardware emulation.

Pure tone, synthesized percussion, sample playback, delay, filtering, arpeggiator and four-track tape are new browser implementations. Tape records mono PCM audio into a tempo-sized loop of up to eight bars (38.4 seconds at 50 BPM). Playback supports half/double speed and reverse. It does not reproduce the six-minute hardware tape, firmware effects, radio, USB modes or all factory synthesis engines. It stores audio only for the current page session. WAV export preserves the loop.

Projects used

  • op1r source a2820a7, Copyright © 2026 pcppcp. GPL-3.0-or-later. Original notices. Original engine files: fmbell.c and supersaw.c.
  • op1kenobi by Alex Ownejazayeri, with Shunya Koide’s HTML/CSS design, provides the 34-column, six-row panel geometry adapted here. The arcade treatment, functional controls and display graphics are newly drawn in HTML/CSS/canvas. MIT notices. Its MP3 kit is not used.
  • op1emu was evaluated as a hardware-emulation route. Its native Blackfin emulator is not included in this browser implementation.
